Hiring Playbook

The goal: a full team of attendants who stay — by hiring all the time, moving fast, and choosing carefully.

We hire for care, detail, timekeeping and honesty. We train the rest.

1

Why we are always hiring

Car wash work has high turnover everywhere. People leave for school, for a better offer, to travel home, or because the work is not for them. That is normal. What hurts us is being caught short — a busy Saturday with two attendants missing and nobody ready to step in.

So we do not open a vacancy when someone leaves. Applications come in all year, we keep good people warm on a bench, and when a space opens we fill it in days, not weeks. Big employers of hourly staff, such as McDonald’s and Chipotle, learnt the same lessons:

  • Applying is easy. Seven questions on a phone, no CV, no email, no fee. Every extra step loses good people.
  • Speed wins. A good applicant takes the first job that calls them back, so every applicant is contacted within one day.
  • The real job is shown, not described. Two days of training beside the team tells us more than any interview, and lets them decide with their eyes open.
  • There is always a bench. Good people without a space yet are the first called when someone leaves.
  • The first 90 days decide who stays. Most people who leave, leave early. Settling them in is part of hiring.
  • Everything is measured. Where good hires come from, and why people leave, decides where effort goes.

4

Reading an application

The Book tags each application to show who to call first.

  • Close by — stays in or near Gwarinpa. A short, cheap journey to work is one of the best signs someone will stay.
  • Car wash experience — helpful, but not required. A careful person with no experience beats a careless one with five years.
  • Fewer days — cannot work 5–6 days a week, 8am to 7pm. This is raised on the call and is usually a no.

Never a reason to hire or reject

  • A CV, school certificate or how well they write.
  • Tribe, religion, state of origin, or who they know.
5

The first call

The first call is short and friendly. It confirms the applicant is real, understands the job, and can get to work on every working day.

Opening line

“Good afternoon, my name is [name] from Jaranow Carwash, 6th Avenue, Gwarinpa. You applied for the car wash attendant job. Is this a good time to talk for five minutes?”

  • The job, plainly: 5–6 days a week, 8am to 7pm, on their feet and in the sun, salary paid monthly.
  • Getting to work: how they will reach 6th Avenue on each working day, and what it will cost them.
  • Motivation: why they want this job, and why they left their last one.
  • Booking training: if it is a fit, two training days are booked in the Book, and the applicant is given the dates, the arrival time (7:45am) and what to wear.
  • Training pay: applicants know before they come whether the training days are paid, and how much.
6

The two training days

Trainees work beside an experienced attendant on real cars. Day one is watching and helping; on day two they wash with the attendant checking. Observations go into the applicant’s notes in the Book.

What is assessed

  • Detail — do they go back for what they missed, or wait to be told?
  • Timekeeping — did they arrive on time both days?
  • Honesty — how do they handle what is inside a customer’s car?
  • Attitude — do they take correction well, and help without being asked?
  • Pace — slow is fine on day one. Careless is not.

The decision is made on the evening of day two and given to the trainee the same day.

7

Hiring and the first 90 days

  • Attendants are offered a monthly salary. Revenue share is only by agreement, if they ask for it and management agrees.
  • Hire in the Book records the salary and adds the new attendant to Staff, ready for attendance and the rota.
  • Before their first day, they read and sign the Employee Handbook and receive the Attendant Guide.
  • Week one: each new attendant is paired with one experienced attendant as their shadow.
  • Day 7, day 30 and day 90: a short private check-in about what is hard, and what can be fixed.
8

When someone leaves

  • The leaver is archived in Staff with their last day and the reason. This is what shows how long people stay, and why they go.
  • Every leaver is asked, kindly, the real reason they are leaving, and the answer is recorded.
  • The bench is called first. New applications are used only if nobody on the bench can come.
9

Rules we never break

  • Applying is free. Nobody at Jaranow asks an applicant for money, a “form fee” or a favour. Anyone who does is dismissed.
  • Never promise what is not confirmed — a salary, an allowance or a day off that management has not agreed.
  • Applicants’ details are private. They are used only for hiring and never shared. Applications that will not be used again are deleted.
  • Everyone gets an answer. Yes, bench, or no — never silence.
10

Monthly review

  • How many applications came in, and from where.
  • How long from application to first call.
  • How many trainees were hired.
  • How many new hires are still on the team after 30 and 90 days, by source.
  • The reasons people left. A reason that keeps coming up is a cause to fix.

Hiring is never finished. A little attention every day keeps the team full and the bench ready.
